Wildfires & digital twins: pitching a safety tech story during a crisis

A timely pitch connecting Alberta wildfire shutdowns to emerging digital twin technology — landed coverage during an active news cycle.

+
Subject: As wildfires force oil site evacuations, this tech is critical to safety Hi [Reporter], Wildfires have now shut-in over 300,000 barrels per day of oil production in Alberta. Cenovus Energy, MEG Energy Corporation and Canadian Natural Resources have all had to evacuate workers from sites. As production is shut-in, workers need a way to effectively plan restarts to operations. Calgary-based VEERUM is creating digital twins — visual representations of site conditions — to help workers access current site conditions from the safety and convenience of the home or office. "By using digital twins, oil companies can plan operations and maintenance activities without travelling to site, cutting down on the number of hours and dollars spent travelling to remote and hazardous sites," says Jordan Mathieson, Marketing Director at VEERUM. "The world's largest asset owners are already adopting this as standard for their critical remote infrastructure," he adds. Can I connect you with Jordan to chat about how digital twins are essential to ensuring safety at oil sites?

The $1 trillion burnout problem — and what companies can do now

A pitch connecting workplace burnout data to a concrete expert voice, building a timely national conversation around mental health at work.

+
Subject: Burnout costs employers $3,400 of every $10,000 in salary: How orgs can adapt Hi [Reporter], One-third of Americans say work is adversely impacting their mental health, and 80% say they feel stress at work. These burned out employees are costing employers $3,400 of every $10,000 in salary as productivity decreases. And depression alone is costing the global economy $1 billion in lost productivity. One corporate wellness expert says companies need to respond to employee needs now — by respecting boundaries and the right to disconnect — before resentment and burnout make top performers leave. "Companies usually wait until it's too late to act on burnout. They need to start embracing preventative solutions," says burnout expert Renée Zavislak, a licensed California-based therapist. "When someone's job security depends on working 60-80 hours a week and taking calls while on vacation, it means companies aren't taking mental health seriously. One solution would be forcing employees to take vacations to recharge. For example, the offices of Duolingo are closed for two weeks over the winter so employees can focus on enjoying the holidays. Those at the top need to model healthy practices." Can I connect you with Renée to chat about how burnout culture is destroying companies, and what leadership can do about it?
